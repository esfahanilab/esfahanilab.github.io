Computational Oncology · Stanford Radiation Oncology

How much of the identity, functional state, and history of cells can we reconstruct from the molecular traces they leave behind?

We build statistical and machine-learning methods that read cell-free DNA, tumor genomes, and tissue images to recover what cells were, what they were doing, and where a cancer is headed, and we translate those readouts into noninvasive tests for detection, response, and residual disease.

The central question

What determines whether an emerging or residual cancer is eliminated, remains controlled, or progresses, and can we recognize and change that trajectory?

Every blood draw carries fragments of DNA released by dying cells, tumor and host alike. Their sequence, their breakpoints, and their nucleosome footprints encode where they came from and what state those cells were in. We treat those fragments as a time-stamped record of the cancer–host equilibrium.

  • ClearedNo detectable disease remains after treatment.
  • ControlledDisease persists but is held in check by therapy or immunity.
  • ProgressesResistant clones emerge and the trajectory bends upward.

How we work

Three ways of reading a molecular trace

Identity

Which cell types, tissues, and tumor subtypes shed the fragments we see? We infer cell-of-origin, genotype, and lineage from fragment ends, methylation-independent nucleosome footprints, and sequence.

Functional state

What were those cells doing? EPIC-Seq and its successors reconstruct gene expression, chromatin accessibility, and proliferation from plasma DNA, turning a liquid biopsy into a liquid transcriptome.

History & trajectory

Where has the disease been, and where is it going? Serial measurements, dynamic risk models, and longitudinal multi-omics let us forecast relapse, resistance, and response in time to act.

Functional state

Gene expression inference from cell-free DNA

Nucleosome positioning at promoters, enhancers, and super-enhancers leaves a fragmentation signature in plasma. We model that signature to infer the expression state of the cells that shed the DNA, and extend it from targeted promoters to the whole regulatory genome.

History & trajectory

Dynamic prognostic and predictive models

Risk is not fixed at diagnosis. Serial biomarkers, interim MRD, and imaging update a patient's risk continuously. We develop models such as CIRI that integrate these measurements to identify who benefits from a given therapy and when to change course.

Identity

Molecular characterization of tumors and their ecosystems

Noninvasive genotyping, phasing, and fragmentomics define new disease subtypes, as in classic Hodgkin lymphoma, and reveal how tumor cells and their immune neighbors are organized in tissue.
